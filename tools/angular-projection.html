Angular Projections

For a given LS combination, the angular distribution I(θ,φ,χ) is expressed as a sum of trigonometric Fourier modes cos(k·θ/2)·cos(m·φ)·sin(n·χ) etc. The θ frequency k is always an integer (half-angle basis). The φ/χ frequencies m, n are integers for integer-spin decays but can be half-integer (m = 1/2, 3/2, …) when helicities are half-integer (as in D1/2). 1D projections are obtained by integrating over all angles except one: \[ I(v) = \int I \prod_{u\neq v} d\mu(u) \] where \(d\mu(\theta_i) = d(\cos\theta_i)\) and \(d\mu(\phi_i) = d\phi_i\), \(d\mu(\chi) = d\chi\). Since the Fourier expansion is a sum of products of single-variable functions, integration reduces each variable independently. The result is a 1D Fourier series in the remaining variable. Normalization: The angular distribution is normalized so that a uniform distribution gives marginal density \(I(\cos\theta_i) = 1/2\) and \(I(\phi_i) = I(\chi) = 1/(2\pi)\). The normalization factor is: \[ \text{norm} = \frac{\prod_i (2J_i+1)}{2^{N_\theta}\, (2\pi)^{N_\phi}} \] where \(N_\theta\) is the number of θ variables and \(N_\phi\) is the number of φ/χ variables, and \(J_i\) is the spin of the mother particle at each decay vertex.
